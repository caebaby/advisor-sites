The year before you become a partner is the most financially consequential of your career. Your income is about to spike. Your tax situation is about to get complicated. And if you don't make the right moves in the right order, you'll spend the next decade undoing expensive mistakes.

Here are five moves that separate the physicians who build lasting wealth from those who earn a lot and keep surprisingly little of it.

## 1. Model Your Tax Exposure Before the Income Hits

Partnership income often comes as a K-1, not a W-2. That means quarterly estimated taxes, self-employment tax on a portion of your income, and potentially a large true-up payment in April if you're not prepared.

> Physicians entering partnership see an average income increase of 40–80% in their first year — and most aren't prepared for the tax bill that follows.

Before your first day as a partner, run a tax projection with your CPA or financial advisor. Know what you'll owe, when you'll owe it, and build a cash reserve specifically for that purpose.

## 2. Max Every Tax-Advantaged Account — In the Right Order

As a partner, you'll likely have access to a 401(k), possibly a profit-sharing plan, and potentially a defined benefit or cash balance plan. The order in which you fund these matters.

### The Right Sequence

1. **401(k) up to the match** (if applicable)
2. **HSA** — the triple-tax advantage account most physicians underuse
3. **Backdoor Roth IRA** — $7,000/year, every year, regardless of income
4. **Max 401(k)** to the IRS limit
5. **Profit sharing / defined benefit plan** if your entity structure supports it

A well-structured physician practice can shelter $80,000–$200,000+ per year in tax-advantaged accounts. Most physicians shelter $23,000. That gap compounds dramatically over 20 years.

## 3. Build a Real Emergency Fund

Your financial risk profile changes at partnership. You now have overhead exposure, personal liability considerations, and a family depending on your income.

Build 6–12 months liquid, in a high-yield savings account you never touch — before you upgrade the house, buy the practice, or increase your lifestyle.

## 4. Get Your Disability Insurance Right

Your most valuable financial asset isn't your portfolio. It's your ability to earn $400K+ per year for the next 25 years.

Key decisions to get right:
- **Own-occupation definition** — covers you if you can't perform your specific specialty
- **Benefit amount** — aim to replace 60–70% of gross income
- **Elimination period** — 90 days is the sweet spot for most physicians
- **Residual/partial disability rider** — covers partial income loss

Most group disability policies through employers are inadequate. Get an independent review before partnership day.

## 5. Write a Real Financial Plan Before You Need One

The worst time to think about financial planning is when something goes wrong. The best time is right now.

A real financial plan answers:
- What does financial independence look like for you, and when could you achieve it?
- Are you on track, and what's the gap if not?
- What happens to your family if you die or become disabled tomorrow?
- What are your tax liabilities in retirement, and how do you minimize them now?

Partnership is the moment when the decisions you make actually matter at scale. Don't make them without a map.
