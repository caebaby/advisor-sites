The window that closes

The decisions you don't make in year one are the hardest ones to fix.

Three months into your first attending job, your student loan grace period ends. Your income is now 3–5x what it was. Your malpractice situation is new. Your tax filing is suddenly complicated. And every product-selling advisor in a 50-mile radius knows exactly when you signed your employment contract.

We built our early-career practice around one idea: meet physicians before the mistakes happen — not after. Because what you do in year one sets the floor for everything that follows.

What we focus on

Six areas that move the needle most

Early-career physicians face a specific set of decisions. We've built our practice around getting these right.

Student Loan Strategy

PSLF, IDR, SAVE, refinancing — this single decision is often worth $100,000–$300,000. The rules changed. If your strategy is more than a year old, it needs to be rebuilt.

Most urgent right now — 6-12 month window
First-Year Cash Flow Plan

Your income tripled overnight. We build a spending plan that protects your future without making you feel like you're living in residency.

Disability & Life Insurance

Physicians are dramatically underinsured at this stage. We'll tell you exactly what you need, what you were sold that you don't need, and where to get coverage without the commission.

Investment Account Sequencing

401(k), Roth IRA, backdoor Roth, HSA — in what order, and how much. Most new attendings have the right accounts open in the wrong order with the wrong investments.

Tax Planning for the Income Surge

Filing is not planning. Your first year as an attending is the most important tax year of your career. We build a proactive strategy before April, not a documentation exercise after.

Estate & Beneficiary Basics

Beneficiary designations on retirement accounts, basic will structure, disability-of-a-parent contingency. Skipped by 90% of new attendings. Costs real money when discovered late.

Common questions

Things we hear all the time

"I'm still in residency — I can't afford a financial planner yet."

The PSLF/SAVE decision alone is a $100,000+ choice that has to be made now, not later. The cost of advice at this stage is almost always smaller than the cost of the wrong decision made without it. We work with residents specifically because of this.

"I'll figure it out once I start making real money."

The decisions you make in your first 90 days as an attending set the baseline for everything. Most are irreversible. The income is real the day you sign — the planning needs to be there the day before.

Great start — genuinely. We've reviewed hundreds of physician plans from people who said the same thing. The books get most things right. They can't account for your specific situation, your loans, your contract, your spouse's income. That's what we do.
