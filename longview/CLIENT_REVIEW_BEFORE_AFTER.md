## Why These Changes

Two issues came up in our compliance review:

1. **NAPFA membership** — NAPFA is a fee-only organization. Because LongView's licensing includes MML Investors Services (a broker-dealer), NAPFA membership cannot be claimed without formal verification. All NAPFA references have been removed.

2. **Fiduciary vs. fee-only framing** — "Fee-only" has a specific regulatory meaning (no commissions of any kind, no BD affiliation). Given LongView's licensing structure, we've reframed from "fee-only" to "fiduciary" — which is accurate, verifiable, and defensible. Fiduciary is also arguably the stronger message: it's the legal standard, not just a compensation structure claim.

3. **Whole life blog post** — The current angle positions whole life insurance as something advisors deceive physicians into buying. Since LongView uses whole life as a legitimate strategy for some clients, this post needs a new angle. **We need your input on this one** before it goes live.

## Category 3 — Whole Life Blog Post (Needs Your Input)

**File:** "The Whole Life Trap" blog post

**The issue:** The current article positions whole life insurance as something advisors deceive physicians into buying. Since LongView recommends whole life in the right circumstances, the article as written would confuse or undermine trust with clients who have been recommended it.

**What we need from you:**
- Confirm LongView's current position on whole life
- Do you want to keep the article (with the reframed angle below) or replace it?

**Suggested new angle:** *"Whole Life Insurance for Physicians: The Right Questions to Ask"*

- Keep the educational content about how whole life works
- Remove language implying agents act in bad faith
- Reframe: whole life can be the right tool in the right situation — the question is whether it's being recommended for the right reasons for your specific situation
- CTA: talk to a fiduciary who will give you an honest evaluation

**We are not publishing this post until you confirm the angle.**
